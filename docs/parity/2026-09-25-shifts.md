# Time-shift ensemble gain on MUSDB18 test previews, 2026-09-25

Hypothesis from the Demucs README: shift ensembling "improves SDR by up
to 0.2 points". Replicated here on the 7-second test previews with the
whole-signal SDR of the MUSDB7 record (not museval; not comparable with
published tables).

Stamp: charon commit `aefd582` (`separate --shifts N`), ort rc.13 / ORT
1.28.0, CPU; PyTorch demucs 4.1.0 with `shifts=1` (one random offset,
`torch.manual_seed(0)`); 50 tracks; `tools/parity/musdb_eval.py`.

charon's shifts are deterministic and evenly spaced in [0, 0.5 s);
Demucs draws random offsets. `shifts=1` in charon means no shift.

| configuration | drums | bass | other | vocals | mean |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| charon shifts 1 (none) | 9.50 | 9.04 | 5.19 | 8.88 | 8.15 |
| charon shifts 2 | 9.53 | 9.20 | 5.21 | 8.82 | 8.19 |
| charon shifts 4 | 9.53 | 9.22 | 5.21 | 8.86 | 8.21 |
| PyTorch shifts 1 (one random offset) | 9.52 | 9.02 | 5.22 | 8.83 | 8.15 |

Gain from shifts on this set: +0.04 to +0.06 dB mean, dominated by bass
(+0.18 dB at 4 shifts); vocals do not improve. Cost: N model runs, so
2x and 4x the separation time. The claimed "up to 0.2" is not reached on
7 s excerpts with this metric; whether full tracks with museval show
more is unmeasured.

Default stays `shifts = 1` (off).
